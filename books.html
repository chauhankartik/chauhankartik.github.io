Key Mental Models Derived from Canonical Literature

Architectural invariants and trade-offs that distinguish senior from staff-level systems design.

Storage Engines (DDIA & Petrov)

LSM-Tree vs B-Tree Duality

LSM-trees turn random writes into sequential disk writes at the cost of compaction overhead and read-amplification. B-trees optimize point reads with deterministic tree depths at the cost of random write I/O.

🃏 Practice Flashcard
Operating Systems (OSTEP)

Futexes & Fast User-Space Locking

Uncontended locks should execute via hardware CAS without a syscall. Kernel context switching into sleep queues only occurs when contention is detected, saving thousands of CPU cycles.

🃏 Practice Flashcard
Concurrency (JCIP & Effective Java)

Happens-Before & Memory Visibility

Synchronization isn't just about atomic mutual exclusion. Without happens-before edges (volatile writes or lock releases), CPU registers and out-of-order execution leave state changes invisible to other cores.

🃏 Practice Flashcard
Distributed Systems (Vitillo & DDIA)

Fencing Tokens & Lease Invalidation

Distributed locks with TTLs (e.g. Redlock) are unsafe against GC pauses unless target storage enforces strictly monotonic fencing tokens on every mutation (`version > prev_version`).
